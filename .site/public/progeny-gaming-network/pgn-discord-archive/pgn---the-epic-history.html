The Epic History of PGN

Progeny Gaming Network, 2015–2026

Working internal history. This was reconstructed from the surviving PGN Discord archive and should be reviewed before any public release. Handles are preserved as they appeared in Discord; passwords, private IPs, tokens, and similar credentials were deliberately excluded.

There are gaming clans built around ladders, tournaments, ranks, recruitment drives, or one immortal game.

PGN is not one of those clans.

The history preserved here is the history of a group that kept finding new excuses to remain in the same room together. Games arrived, briefly became the most important thing in the world, and then disappeared. Servers were built, rebuilt, broken, abandoned, revived, renamed, and occasionally resurrected years after anyone sensible would have left them alone. Projects that began as jokes became software. Old software became nostalgia. Nostalgia became new projects.

The games were temporary.

The people were the persistent world.

The surviving archive contains 68,975 unique Discord messages across the exported channels and threads. 62,498 of those messages were written by 193 distinct human author IDs; the remaining 6,477 came from bots. The record begins on October 19, 2016 and runs through September 27, 2026. PGN’s own 2026 description says the clan itself dates to 2015, so the Discord record begins after the story was already underway.

One archival problem deserves to be stated immediately: a single now-deleted Discord account accounts for 8,114 surviving messages. The words remain, but the identity does not. Any history built from the archive therefore contains a large person-shaped blank spot.

And one other warning: the old chat is old internet chat. It contains the jokes, insults, language, moods, and questionable decisions of the people who wrote it at the time. This history preserves the shape of that culture without trying to reproduce every slur or every argument.

The raw numbers tell one story before a single message is read. PGN’s surviving Discord record starts hot, explodes in 2017, remains extremely active through 2018, dips hard in 2019, surges again in 2020, reaches its quietest full year in 2021, and then begins a slow second life. By 2024 the server is busier than it had been since 2018, but the shape of the activity has changed: instead of one giant general channel carrying everything, dedicated game threads, server spaces, project channels, and later the Workshop begin preserving individual eras.

That structural change is important. Early PGN history is a river. Later PGN history has tributaries with names.

Prologue — Before the Archive: The Old Net

The earliest surviving Discord message is not the beginning of PGN.

PGN’s own 2026 about-pgn post calls the community “est. 2015” and says it began as a dedicated clan for Multi Theft Auto and Minecraft. [E42] That description is the strongest explicit statement inside the archive about the organization’s starting point.

There is also an older lineage preserved as clan lore. In October 2025, while working on a deliberately over-the-top PGN song, Shagwrath wrote the sequence:

Hell Soldiers → Helvete → WSS → PGN. [E37]

Because that sequence appears inside a stylized retrospective song rather than a contemporaneous 2015 record, it belongs in the category of oral tradition rather than hard archival chronology. But it matches something the Discord record makes obvious: Helvete, WSS, and the wider MTA scene were not random references. They were old countries people could still point to on the map.

By 2018, Shagwrath was talking about a future Helvete tattoo specifically because he wanted a permanent reminder of the MTA years and the friends attached to them. [E10] In 2020, people could still debate WSS history, old scripts, old HUDs, old server culture, and who had been where. By 2022, the MTA ecosystem had become a philosophical subject: not just a game people once played, but an example of how a community can outlive the software that created it.

That is the real prehistory of PGN. Before Discord became the archive, there were already servers, factions, scripts, maps, in-jokes, friendships, rivalries, and names with baggage.

PGN did not begin from nothing.

It condensed out of previous worlds.

Chapter I — 2016: “Harro”

The surviving Discord age begins on October 19, 2016 at 4:33 PM.

Tempa writes:

Harro

Thirty seconds later, Shagwrath answers:

yo [E01]

This is, objectively, an excellent founding document.

There is no proclamation. No constitution. No heroic call to arms. Within minutes the channel is emojis, colors, insults, jokes, and people figuring out what Discord can do. The archive’s first evening already looks less like a newly created organization and more like an existing friend group moving furniture into a new house.

The first partial year alone contains 5,542 human messages. That is an enormous amount of chatter for an archive that starts in late October.

Overwatch, MTA, and the first Discord identity

A day after the archive begins, there is already a reference to a “progeny Overwatch team.” [E02] Overwatch appears dozens of times before the end of the year. The group was not entering Discord with MTA as its only active identity; it was already hopping between games.

But MTA was still cultural bedrock.

On November 1, Shagwrath writes:

Hello. My name is Shagwrath, and I play MTA. [E03]

The joke immediately reproduces itself through other members. The archive is full of that pattern: one person says something dumb, the group grabs it, repeats it, mutates it, and years later someone remembers it.

By early November, PGN Admin and PGN Moderator roles are already being invoked. Later that month, automated welcome messages are greeting new arrivals with the full name Progeny Gaming Network. There were designs for PGN clothing, a Steam presence, ideas about logos, and all the other little signs that the group still understood itself as a clan rather than simply a Discord server.

And yet the strongest statement of identity in 2016 is not a role or logo.

On Christmas Day, PcBro Stig posted a message telling everyone they were the best friends a guy could ask for, joking about Overwatch and saying MTA had nothing on them. [E04]

That is only two months into the surviving archive, and already the central conflict of PGN history has been resolved.

The clan branding mattered. The games mattered. The friendship mattered more.

Chapter II — 2017: The Year PGN Exploded

If 2016 is the arrival, 2017 is the detonation.

The archive contains 18,817 human messages from 2017, by far the highest total of any year in the surviving record. March alone is a torrent. General chat is not merely active; it is the operating system for a large chunk of daily social life.

This is the year when PGN looks most like the classic idea of an online gaming clan: people are constantly calling one another into games, asking where everyone is, running or discussing servers, renaming themselves with clan tags, redesigning branding, dragging friends into whatever the new obsession happens to be, and occasionally wandering into another clan before wandering back.

The ARK Winter

January opens with an ARK phase strong enough to leave clear server-administration evidence. Shagwrath asks whether SGN has an ARK server; soon after, there are questions about server IPs, updates, RCON, console commands, creatures, and whether the server is down. [E05]

The most convincing proof that a group is genuinely playing ARK is not a server config. It is sleep deprivation.

PcBro Stig describes staying up until seven in the morning taming a bird, sleeping for two hours, and discovering that the world had not respected his labor.

That is ARK.

The PUBG Spring and Summer

By April, PUBG starts appearing. By May it becomes a recurring summons:

Anyone up for pubg 2nite? [E06]

Through spring and summer, the archive repeatedly contains versions of the same ancient multiplayer ritual: PUBG? Anyone? @everyone PUBG? Pubg later?

It is one of the clearest game eras in the early archive because the messages are not merely links or opinions. They are attempts to assemble the group in real time.

FiveM: The Server Instinct Returns

At almost the same moment, the old MTA/server-building instinct is looking for a new body.

In April, FiveM links appear. In May someone asks whether the FiveM server is still happening. By June, Juby says he thinks “they are working on a FiveM server.” [E07]

This will become one of the repeating patterns of PGN history: when the current game landscape fails to provide exactly the experience the group wants, somebody starts wondering whether PGN should host or build the experience itself.

Everything Else, Constantly

ARK and PUBG are only the easiest periods to prove. 2017 also contains Rust, Overwatch, Battlefront, Counter-Strike, Factorio, Fortnite, Minecraft, and other shorter-lived interests. Games overlap instead of forming clean dynasties. A title can dominate for a week, disappear, and return months later. Someone can refund the game everyone else is currently trying to organize. Somebody else buys in late.

PGN’s historical pattern is already visible:

  1. Somebody discovers or rediscovers a game.
  2. Links appear.
  3. A few people become evangelists.
  4. @everyone is deployed as a blunt instrument.
  5. A server is considered, rented, configured, or fantasized about.
  6. The group either settles in or collectively wanders away.

The Logo, the Clan, and ClanHopperWrath

In July, Shagwrath asks everyone to design a new Progeny logo because the server icon needs to be remade. [E08] It is a tiny moment, but it shows that the identity is still formal enough to care about visual branding.

Then comes one of the best little bits of institutional history in the archive.

In December, Shagwrath asks:

can i come back to the clan? whose ass do i gotta lick [E09]

The following conversation establishes that he had left PGN earlier that year and formed another clan with Heady, Elle, and Amir. When that adventure ended, he labeled himself:

#ClanHopperWrath

And PGN, because PGN is PGN, absorbed the whole thing into the joke stream.

That is the other secret of the community’s survival. Leaving does not always mean disappearing. Membership is porous. People drift. People return. Identity stretches instead of snapping.

Chapter III — 2018: Builders, Servers, and the Memory of Helvete

2018 remains extremely active, with 8,939 human messages, but the year feels different from 2017. It is less like one giant explosion and more like a series of concentrated obsessions.

Crossout: Recruitment by Harassment

March belongs to Crossout.

The campaign begins simply:

@everyone get crossout awesome vehicle combat game, free. me and amir need players [E11]

What follows is a sustained recruitment campaign. Shagwrath asks Juby, Steven, KouseiRyu, Choco, and anyone else in range whether they want to play. Crossout is described as vehicle combat, leveling, and building — essentially a bridge between PGN’s love of systems, customization, and chaos.

Crossout will not remain confined to 2018. It resurfaces later, especially in 2023, making it one of those games that never quite becomes the permanent PGN game but never fully leaves the collective memory either.

7 Days to Die: The Server Is Never Enough

In May, the archive records another classic PGN sentence:

got a 7 days to die server if anyoens interested [E12]

Within two weeks, merely having the server is already insufficient. The conversation turns toward adding a scripting mod so the server can support custom behavior.

This is a recurring PGN disease.

A normal group asks, What game are we playing?

PGN eventually asks, What can we change about the game?

Helvete Becomes Memory

Also in May, Helvete is discussed not as a current game state but as a memory worth preserving. Shagwrath talks about getting a Helvete tattoo because he wants something permanent that will remind him of the fun, the MTA era, and the friends. [E10]

This is one of the first places where the archive becomes consciously historical. The group is only a few years removed from those old MTA experiences, but already understands that an era has ended.

Dargeath: A Lost World

Then there is Dargeath, the old Minecraft RPG project.

In January, an old Minecraft forum link resurfaces. People remember it. In February: “i miss dargeath.”

In July, the bad news arrives: the server backups appear to have been lost in a crash.

rip Dargeath [E13]

Three days later Soul echoes the funeral. Two weeks later Shagwrath is making a Dargeath map because he is bored.

That sequence could serve as a summary of the next eight years of PGN history:

The old project is dead.
RIP old project.
Anyway, I started rebuilding it.

Chapter IV — 2019: The Taco Shack Interregnum

2019 looks strange in the statistics.

There are 3,686 human-authored messages, but a large fraction are not really conversation at all. A simple command-pattern pass identifies roughly 2,200 command-like messages during the year. !work appears hundreds of times. So does !tip. !shack becomes its own miniature language.

This is the year of Taco Shack.

On June 11, Shagwrath announces that the Discord server has been reorganized, with channels added, removed, and rearranged. [E14] Six minutes later:

Added a new game, taco shack bots commands are in the channel! [E15]

And the clan proceeds to attack the bot economy with exactly the seriousness one would expect.

This is why raw message counts can mislead. June 2019 is one of the largest months in the archive, but much of that volume is humans repeatedly commanding bots.

Still, the year matters.

The Discord itself is being maintained and reorganized. PGN has not vanished simply because there is no dominant new shared game. The clan’s digital house is being cleaned even during a quieter gaming period.

There are also periodic attempts to reconnect identity with the past. In January, Shagwrath suggests simply rebranding the clan to Helvete. The suggestion does not become the permanent identity, but the instinct is revealing: when PGN wonders what it is, it often searches backward through its own history.

2019 is therefore less an empty year than an intermission.

The stage remains occupied. Everyone is messing with the props.

Chapter V — 2020: Return to the Roots, Then Turn Away

2020 brings 5,170 human messages, a significant rebound.

It is one of the most important years in the archive because PGN spends the year looking backward and forward at the same time.

MTA Becomes Ancient History While People Are Still Playing It

In February, s1nc3 observes that the previous three or four years feel like an eternity and that MTA already feels like a major throwback. [E16]

That is remarkable. MTA is not yet some artifact from a distant childhood. The people involved are still capable of reinstalling it, reopening projects, arguing over old server communities, and working with old assets. Yet emotionally it has already crossed the line into the old days.

March and April contain a genuine MTA mini-revival: old roleplay servers are discussed, old WSS material is examined, old UI is identified, people return to the game, and Shagwrath begins experimenting with MTA systems again.

The largest idea is Hogwarts RP.

Thinking of making an mta server. Hogwarts RP like that gmod server we all played on. [E17]

That project leads into map work, physics experiments, objects, and the familiar feeling that perhaps one more MTA project could recapture the magic.

August 29: The Pivot

Then, on August 29, the archive catches an unusually clean historical turning point.

At 2:57 AM, Shagwrath says in general:

this is why i’m just gonna make games in unity now

Seconds later, the news channel announces that he has created a video game company and is building a community around an original hand-drawn platformer. [E18]

Later that day, the Hogwarts server comes up again. Shagwrath explains that mod projects keep dying, forcing creators to move to another mod and start over. Then comes the sentence that bridges the entire first half of PGN history to the second:

if it werent for mta i wouldnt have learned to script so it had its place, time to move on, for real [E19]

That is not the end of MTA. Nothing in PGN ever ends that neatly.

It is, however, the moment when the archive explicitly reframes MTA from destination to origin story.

The game had taught skills that could now escape the game.

Approximately one minute later, Shagwrath asks who wants to play Grounded.

This is also very PGN.

The Dargeath Problem Never Goes Away

In December, conversation returns to Dargeath. The old project gets the gears turning again:

it’s making me want to find something we could all team up on like old times [E20]

This is the defining desire underneath nearly every later PGN server.

Not necessarily this exact game.

Not necessarily this exact mod.

Something everyone can build, inhabit, and remember together.

Chapter VI — 2021: The Quiet Workshop

2021 is the quietest full year in the surviving archive: 1,619 human messages.

But quiet is not the same as inactive.

The clan begins to look less like a game-specific group and more like a place where unfinished ideas are allowed to live until somebody becomes obsessed with them again.

Safe Cracker Returns

On March 7, Shagwrath asks whether people remember his Safe Cracker game — originally associated with an SAES bank-robbing trainer — and wonders how it would work on a Game Boy. [E21]

The group debates controls. A D-pad seems too slow. Maybe a keypad. Maybe the concept does not fit the hardware. Maybe the challenge is exactly why it is worth doing.

By June, there is talk of making a new version and putting it on Steam. By September, there is a prototype, multiple game modes, story-mode planning, playtester recruitment, and the recurring development problem of making a mechanically functional concept into a complete game.

Safe Cracker will continue to mutate for five more years.

Infrastructure Returns Too

2021 also contains a small but important server milestone:

we have a webhost again! we can host servers and shit yay [E22]

There are Windows server problems, dead download links, virtualization confusion, dedicated-server wiping, and the familiar joy of infrastructure finally working.

The games may be changing, but the server-admin instinct is permanent.

Missing the Old Days

The emotional tone of the year is frequently nostalgic.

Yuu writes:

I miss.mta days. It was so much easier.. [E23]

Shagwrath wonders whether not wanting to play games anymore means he is getting old, and later complains that there are plenty of good games but too few truly exceptional ones.

That feeling becomes an engine for PGN’s later history. If the perfect game does not exist, revive an old one. If the old one is dead, host it yourself. If that does not work, make something.

2021 is not a dead year.

It is the year the Workshop exists before there is a channel called The Workshop.

Chapter VII — 2022: The Game Is the Medium

The archive climbs again in 2022 to 2,756 human messages.

This is the year PGN accidentally explains itself.

March 19: The Thesis

During a conversation about SAES, old players, old hardware, and whether an aging game community can migrate to something newer, Steven writes:

i feel like its more of just a place to interact with a community, the game being the medium [E24]

A few messages later:

everyone is still ‘around’ just not ingame really

That is the best single-sentence description of PGN in the entire archive.

The history makes more sense if every game is treated as a medium rather than the organization itself.

MTA was a medium.
Minecraft was a medium.
ARK was a medium.
Discord eventually became a medium.
Later, servers and home-built activities became mediums.

The persistent object was the social network.

Grounded Summer

In August, Grounded becomes one of the most clearly organized play periods of the middle years. The archive fills with repeated invitations aimed at Steven, Yuu, Juby, and others. A shared-world feature is celebrated because it means the group can use the same world even when the host is offline. [E25]

There are evening sessions, resource-gathering sessions, one-hour sessions, and a 4:30 AM session.

That is no longer a formal competitive clan behavior.

It is friends maintaining a shared place.

Valheim Winter

By late November, attention drifts toward Valheim.

Tilong eventually signs off after playing for approximately sixteen hours in one day. [E26]

Before the year ends, the question is already being asked: should PGN start a Valheim server?

The answer, historically, is yes.

It just takes a while to arrive.

Chapter VIII — 2023: Old Obsessions Learn New Tricks

2023 records 2,869 human messages. The group is not exploding in size, but it is remarkably good at generating new mini-eras.

Crossout, Again

January and February bring a deliberate Crossout revival.

Shagwrath pitches it to Tilong as Twisted Metal where you build your own car, openly admitting the pay-to-win feeling but insisting effort can overcome it. [E27]

Soon the discussion includes whether PGN should start a Crossout clan.

Crossout has now completed a full PGN lifecycle:

new obsession → old game → revived obsession → clan idea

ARK, Again

March brings ARK back into view. Steven says he might be down to play again. [E28]

The word again is doing a lot of work in this history.

FiveM, Again — But as a Build

By July, FiveM is no longer merely a server someone might spin up. Shagwrath and Nick are working on a FiveM zombie server, saying the core is nearly ready and mapping will begin soon. [E29]

This is the later PGN pattern in mature form: old MTA instincts, newer GTA infrastructure, custom systems, and development work blended together.

Mordhau: Summoning the Missing

Also in July:

@Steven where the fuck be you for mordhauin [E30]

It is difficult to write a more concise description of PGN matchmaking.

The Next Survival Game Is Always Coming

Valheim remains in the background. ARK 2 is discussed. Enshrouded begins attracting attention late in the year, including speculation that it might replace Valheim if the server can run it.

The group is perpetually searching for the next shared world.

Sometimes it finds one.

Sometimes the search itself is the activity.

Chapter IX — 2024: The Threaded Renaissance

2024 is the largest human-message year since 2018, with 5,831 messages.

More importantly, Discord organization finally begins matching how PGN actually behaves.

On February 12, the new games space starts breaking individual games into threads. The first Skull and Bones thread opens with:

Trying out threads instead! [E31]

Within roughly two weeks, PGN has dedicated spaces for Skull and Bones, Palworld, Lethal Company, Enshrouded, Star Wars Battlefront, and then Helldivers II.

This is not merely cosmetic organization. It changes the historical record. Instead of every obsession dissolving into #general, later game eras leave named ruins.

February: The Great Thread Bloom

In 2024 alone:

  • Palworld records 75 human messages in its thread.
  • Skull and Bones records 49.
  • Star Wars Battlefront records 86.
  • Enshrouded and Lethal Company receive their own spaces as well.

The server itself is becoming a museum while the events are still happening.

March–May: Helldivers II

Then Helldivers II hits.

Its thread records 595 human messages in 2024, dwarfing the other dedicated game threads. Shagwrath, Yuu, Steven, BigChungus, s1nc3, Soul, keith, Heady and others appear throughout the conversation.

The peak days contain dozens of messages. The thread remains busy through March, April, and May, then tapers.

This is one of the strongest later-period examples of PGN truly converging on a single game.

Minecraft, Because Of Course

On March 25, Shagwrath announces a Minecraft Java Vanilla server, saying maybe it will become a larger project but at minimum they are going to have some fun. [E32]

Within days there is a villager breeder and the declaration:

If it can be farmed from a mob, it will be.

Some traditions are more durable than institutions.

Safe Cracker Becomes a Released Game

In August, the games channel gets a Steam link for Safe Cracker, with keys offered to the group and an announced August 31 release. [E33]

The idea visible in the archive as an old SAES trainer, a possible Game Boy project, and a Unity prototype is now an actual Steam product.

That is a long arc:

old server minigame → nostalgic prototype → game-dev experiment → released game

PGN’s history is now producing artifacts outside PGN.

The Late-Year Carousel

The year closes with more named mini-eras: old-school Minecraft, Phasmophobia, Valheim/Bog Witch, The Long Drive, Voidtrain, and whatever else someone has found on Steam this week.

The difference is that they now leave labeled strata.

Future archaeologists will not need to guess nearly as much.

Chapter X — 2025: The Server-Hopping Age

2025 contains 3,997 human messages, but the number alone understates how concentrated several game periods are.

January–February: Valheim Becomes a Place

The dedicated Valheim server thread records 429 human messages between January 20 and February 25.

This is not merely discussion of Valheim. It is settlement.

People debate resource gathering, building, copper, houses, shadows, villages, and the sort of monumental architectural decisions that always seem completely reasonable after several consecutive nights in a survival game.

The thread ends with a conversation about a structure so large that it throws a shadow across somebody else’s house.

Civilization has been achieved.

March: The Isle Coordinate Epoch

For about one week in March, The Isle thread produces 179 messages.

The archive turns into navigation data.

Coordinates are posted, pinned, updated, shared, and used to regroup. [E34] There are food spots, player locations, map links, and constant attempts to find one another.

It is a compact but perfect game era: intense, practical, social, and then suddenly over.

Spring and Summer: RoadCraft, Avorion, and the Next Thing

May brings RoadCraft. June brings an Avorion server, pitched to Tilong as a mix of Eve Online, Space Engineers, and X4. [E35]

There are already enormous ships and rail guns by the time people are still deciding whether to buy the game.

This is another mature PGN pattern: server first, onboarding second.

October: PGN Writes Its Own Myth

In October, PGN becomes self-consciously historical again.

Shagwrath works on a PGN rap/song whose lyrics explicitly tell a mythic version of the clan’s lineage: Hell Soldiers, Helvete, WSS, PGN, followed by MTA, Minecraft, old members, old jokes, jobs, age, and the fact that everyone somehow never completely logged out. [E37]

The song should not be treated as a literal factual transcript of pre-Discord events. It is more valuable than that.

It is evidence that by 2025 the clan has developed an oral tradition.

The past is no longer merely remembered.

It is being narrated.

December: Minecraft Comes Home Again

On December 16, the Minecraft server thread opens. The address being shared is on helvete.pizza, neatly folding an ancient clan name into modern infrastructure.

Steven responds:

it may be time for another minecraft run [E36]

That sentence could have been posted in nearly any PGN era.

The server is Java. People are already established. The Ender Dragon has already been beaten. A Dargeath RPG-ish project appears again before the year closes.

Dargeath, once mourned because its backups were lost, is not merely remembered anymore.

It is a version number.

Chapter XI — 2026: PGN Becomes a Lab

The archive ends on September 27, so 3,272 human messages represent only part of 2026.

Yet this partial year contains one of the most dramatic transformations in the whole record.

PGN still plays games.

But increasingly, PGN also builds the things around the games.

The Workshop

The shagwraths-shit channel — later sitting under The Workshop — records 574 human messages in 2026.

Its first 2026 message is a FiveM slot machine, “the start of it at least.” [E38]

Very quickly the channel becomes an ongoing lab: web experiments, game systems, personal projects, code, websites, tools, and prototypes get dropped in front of the same people who once spent entire evenings yelling for one more PUBG player.

The feedback loop is familiar.

The medium has changed.

Dune: Awakening — Dedicated Server, Naturally

In May, Dune server work begins surfacing in the PGN Servers area. There are settings, connectivity problems, port-forwarding problems, private-server experiments, sandstorm mechanics, and later a customized PGN server identity. [E39]

In June, a game update breaks servers and becomes another infrastructure problem to solve.

By July, PGN is publicly describing the server and its tuned rules.

A clan born around custom multiplayer spaces has returned, eleven years later, to custom multiplayer spaces.

The tools are simply more complicated now.

Star Wars: Battlefront II — Resurrecting 2005

Then PGN performs one of its purest acts of historical self-expression:

It launches a dedicated server for Star Wars: Battlefront II (Classic, 2005).

The July announcement names the server:

PGN – Ewok Around and Find Out [E40]

By September, that simple server has already grown a landing page and leaderboard. The roadmap includes player identity, lifetime statistics, XP, levels, ranks, awards, achievements, an economy, gambling, custom classes, persistent campaigns, Discord account linking, daily challenges, and dynamic difficulty. [E41]

This is the entire PGN development instinct in miniature.

Step one: host an old game.

Step two: decide it needs a civilization.

Safe Cracker Becomes a Discord Activity

The same month, Safe Cracker mutates again.

A dedicated activity channel appears. A Wordle-style daily structure, leaderboard, visual work, sound, testing, Discord verification, and launch requirements are discussed. [E44]

The thing that began as an MTA/SAES-era safe-cracking concept has now crossed MTA, Game Boy thinking, Unity, Steam, and Discord’s embedded activity platform.

Safe Cracker is not merely a project.

It is a fossil that keeps evolving.

PGN Finally Writes the About Page

On September 10, an about-pgn post formalizes what the archive has been demonstrating for a decade:

PGN formed around 2015. It began around MTA and Minecraft. It has hosted countless servers and projects. Some games lasted a weekend and some lasted years. And these days, it says, the group is “more of a group of friends than a formal clan.” [E42]

This is not the death of the clan identity.

It is the clan identity finally describing what it had already become.

A server-status board appears the same day, cataloging current, paused, and offline servers. [E43] The Graveyard preserves names of old activities and games. The Discord has become a historical structure: living projects above, dead projects below, old roles still visible, new experiments everywhere.

The Last Day in the Archive

The final full day captured here is September 27, 2026.

There is Wordle.

There are videos and memes.

There is a link to a lab page documenting instruments.

There is a conversation about whether Minecraft used to feel more like horror: fog, darkness, skeletons, zombies, enormous empty worlds, the loneliness of early survival games.

Then, at 8:45 PM, the final archived message arrives.

It is not written by a person.

It is an automated welcome message:

Welcome to Progeny Gaming Network, @PGN Archivist! [E45]

The bot created to preserve PGN’s history becomes the final line of the history it just preserved.

There was no possible way to plan a better ending.

Interlude — The People in the Record

Message count is not rank, value, status, or friendship. It only tells us who left a large textual footprint in the surviving archive.

Still, the footprint is useful.

Several patterns are visible immediately.

Shagwrath, Steven, and Juby are present at the beginning of the Discord record and still appear years later. PcBro Stig, Choco, s1nc3, Yuu, Headygr, Amir, SlayerZim, Tempa, and Byars all connect the early era to different portions of the later record. Soul becomes a major presence from 2018 onward. This matters because PGN does not have one clean “generation change.” The old guard does not vanish and get replaced. Instead, the active cast gradually shifts while older figures continue reappearing.

The result is less like a roster and more like a long-running television show where nobody is entirely sure who counts as a recurring character because somebody can disappear for three seasons and then walk back into the episode.

The Recurring Laws of PGN History

After ten years of messages, certain laws appear to govern the universe.

Law One: Every Game Is Temporary

PGN repeatedly behaves as if this one might be the game everyone stays in.

It never is.

That is not failure. It is the mechanism.

New games create bursts of energy. People build something together. The game cools down. The social network remains available for the next burst.

The lack of a permanent game is one reason PGN survives.

Law Two: A Server Is a Campfire

Dedicated servers appear throughout the history because they create a place that belongs to the group.

An ARK server is not just ARK.

A Valheim server is not just Valheim.

A Minecraft server is not just Minecraft.

It is a shared location with a password, a map, a history, somebody’s ridiculous building, somebody else’s lost inventory, and a set of events that make no sense to outsiders.

That is why PGN keeps hosting.

Law Three: Dead Projects Are Not Dead

Dargeath dies and returns.

MTA dies and returns.

Helvete dies and returns as a name, a memory, a website domain, or a possible rebrand.

Battlefront II is twenty-one years old and gets a development roadmap.

Safe Cracker crosses multiple platforms and incarnations.

PGN does not maintain a normal boundary between dead and alive.

There is only:

active,
inactive, and
somebody is thinking about it again.

Law Four: The Joke Is Infrastructure

The archive is saturated with running jokes, nonsense names, sexual humor, insults between friends, cursed channel names, memes, deliberately stupid server names, and phrases that only make sense because somebody said something ten years ago.

This is not decoration around the history.

It is part of the continuity system.

Games disappear. Jokes migrate.

Law Five: Playing Eventually Becomes Making

The technical arc is one of the clearest long-term changes in the archive.

Early PGN operates and modifies multiplayer servers.

Then people script more complicated behavior.

Then the conversation moves toward custom GTA/FiveM systems.

Then Unity and independent games.

Then web hosting, GitHub, custom server tooling, dashboards, Discord bridges, embedded activities, statistics, economies, and persistent systems.

The clan does not stop gaming.

It gradually becomes a group that can build the machinery around its games.

Law Six: “The Game Being the Medium”

Steven’s 2022 sentence deserves to stand as the thesis of the whole history:

a place to interact with a community, the game being the medium

PGN’s games are not trivial. They create the stories.

But they are media through which the group continues to exist.

That is why the community can survive the death of MTA, the loss of Dargeath, a bad ARK patch, the end of a Valheim run, a disappointing release, adulthood, jobs, moves, relationships, long absences, and years where almost nothing seems to happen.

The game can end.

The channel is still there.

Epilogue — The Clan That Time Forgot

One of the 2025 joke songs calls PGN “the clan that time forgot.”

It is a good line, but it is wrong in one important way.

Time did not forget PGN.

PGN kept forgetting to die.

It should have ended when people moved on from MTA.

It should have ended when Minecraft worlds disappeared.

It should have ended when old servers died, when people got jobs, when game tastes changed, when members vanished, when Discord got quiet, when the old clan model stopped making sense.

Instead, it kept changing definitions.

A clan became a friend group.

A friend group became a server community.

A server community became a place to show projects.

A place to show projects became a workshop.

The workshop began producing new games and new servers based on memories of the old ones.

And eventually, after roughly a decade of Discord history, PGN created a bot whose job was to preserve everything.

The final line in the exported archive is that bot being welcomed into the server.

Welcome to Progeny Gaming Network, @PGN Archivist!

So this is not the ending.

It is simply the point where the historians arrived.

Research Notes

This draft was generated from the DiscordChatExporter JSON archive supplied on September 28, 2026.

What is strongly supported

  • Discord dates, channel/thread names, authors, message counts, and message contents.
  • Major game periods where repeated direct play invitations, server operations, or dedicated-thread activity exist.
  • The 2026 PGN self-description that dates the group to 2015 and identifies MTA/Minecraft roots.
  • Project and server launches recorded directly in PGN channels.

What is less certain

  • Events before October 19, 2016, because they predate the surviving Discord record.
  • The precise historical sequence Hell Soldiers → Helvete → WSS → PGN. It is preserved in a 2025 retrospective song and should be treated as clan oral history until corroborated by older material.
  • Membership status inferred from activity alone. Posting in PGN is not proof of a particular formal rank or membership status.
  • Any “era” boundary. Games overlap, return, and coexist.

Important archival gaps

  • One deleted Discord account contributes 8,114 messages and cannot be reliably named from the export.
  • Deleted channels can survive as references without their original messages.
  • Voice chat itself is not recorded; only text written in voice-channel chats is available.
  • The archive records what people typed, not every game session that occurred.
  • Store links prove interest or discussion, not necessarily that the clan adopted a game.

For the message-level research trail, see PGN - Historical Evidence Ledger.md in the companion files.
