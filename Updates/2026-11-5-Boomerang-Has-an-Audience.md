# Boomerang Has an Audience, and It Isn't Just Children

I got BOOM1 built. Getting there involved a large pile of cartoons, another pile of channel branding, and a reminder that cartoons do not automatically mean toy commercials.

## The station between the shows

The new Yogi Bear, Huckleberry Hound, Quick Draw McGraw, and Tom and Jerry files gave me enough material to make two distinct Boomerang carts. The next job was finding the little bits that make them feel like television instead of a playlist.

I found a bulk collection of individual Boomerang bumpers, idents, and promos on Internet Archive. I downloaded the files there directly, which worked out fine. Separate clips are the important part. I have no interest in spending an evening manually cutting a hundred bumpers out of somebody's enormous compilation.

After reviewing the candidates, I had 37 approved branding clips to work with. That included the toy box imagery, little character figures, and show announcements that give this era of Boomerang its particular feel.

The first preparation attempt stopped because the branding approval file was being interpreted as containing unapproved clips. That got corrected, and the check could finally show me the proposed cart before reserving anything.

## Four blocks of old cartoons

BOOM1 has three show blocks each of The Flintstones, The Jetsons, Yogi Bear, and Quick Draw McGraw. Rather than constantly rotating through all four series, it spends some time with each one before moving on.

Some of the shorter cartoons needed to be assembled into longer blocks. Those composites let the shorts fit into the same scheduling system as the full episodes, while the branding provides the transitions around them.

The initial cart had a couple of problems. There were consecutive Flintstones announcements at the start, and I wasn't happy with the commercial choices. Even with general products in the mix, the overall selection felt too much like another children's cartoon cart.

This is Boomerang. A substantial part of the appeal is adults deliberately watching cartoons they remember. I wanted the breaks to reflect that, with more ordinary household, restaurant, automotive, retail, and service advertising.

The correction replaced the eleven commercials with choices from the general audience pool and removed the redundant announcements. It also preserved the review work I'd already done. Having to approve the same crops and clips again would have been a particularly stupid reward for noticing a problem.

BOOM1 is finished. BOOM2 is next, and the no-repeat rule still applies to the whole project: episodes, commercials, bumpers, promos, and idents. I am apparently running a television network with a very small screen and an unusually strict accounting department.
