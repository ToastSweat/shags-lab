I Accidentally Downloaded 131GB of Television

Last time I wrote about this project I had figured out how to make my tiny retro television play custom videos from USB drives.

That was the important breakthrough.
The USB drives could be little cartridges.
Not just one show per drive either.
I wanted fake television stations.

Nickelodeon.
Cartoon Network.
Kids' WB.
Fox Kids.
Sci-Fi.
UPN.
HBO.
AMC.

Twenty little 4GB flash drives pretending to be cable television.

At the end of the last update I had one problem.
I needed commercials.
A lot of commercials.

Because this:

```text
Rugrats
Hey Arnold!
CatDog
Doug
```

is a playlist.

This:

```text
Rugrats
Fruit Roll-Ups commercial
Hey Arnold!
Nintendo commercial
CatDog
Pizza Hut commercial
Doug
PSA
```

is television.

So I started collecting old commercials.

This got out of hand almost immediately.

I found a few Internet Archive collections with individually clipped 90s commercials, giant collections of general American TV commercials, old Nickelodeon and Cartoon Network recordings, late-night stuff, promos, bumpers, and whatever else people had rescued from VHS tapes over the years.

At first I thought I would just grab maybe 120 or 150 commercials.

That was adorable.
The first commercial library ended up with hundreds of candidates.
And then I discovered the real problem.
I had to actually look at them.

There is apparently no useful way to look at a directory containing hundreds of files named things like:

```text
commercial_103.mp4
BK_1997_02.mp4
nick_break_4.mp4
```

and decide which ones belong on fake Nickelodeon without slowly losing your mind.

So naturally I made a tool.

I wrote a PowerShell scanner that walks the archive, calls `ffprobe`, and records things like:

```text
duration
video codec
audio codec
resolution
frame rate
sample rate
year
source collection
filename
```

It also tries to tell the difference between an individual commercial, a short break, a bumper, and a giant source recording.

That gave me a CSV.

Which was better.
But I still had hundreds of video files to review.
So then I made another tool.
The second script generates thumbnails with FFmpeg and builds a local HTML review site.

Now instead of opening random files in VLC one at a time, I get a giant wall of old television garbage.

Perfect.

Every clip has a thumbnail, filename, duration, source, preview button, category dropdown, and notes field.

I can tag things as:

```text
KIDS_90S
KIDS_00S
GENERAL_90S
GENERAL_00S
LATE_NIGHT
PSA
PROMO
BUMPER
IDENT
NEEDS_CLIPPED
SKIP
```

The browser saves my progress locally and I can export the finished curation to CSV.

This is one of those little side tools that probably took longer to build than just suffering through the files manually once.

But now I can do this forever.
Which is much more dangerous.
The first review pass had 411 logical assets.
I reviewed every single one.

The final pile included:

```text
102 KIDS_90S
40  GENERAL_90S
7   GENERAL_00S
11  LATE_NIGHT
10  PSA
48  PROMO
15  BUMPER
1   IDENT
8   NEEDS_CLIPPED
169 SKIP
```

That was already enough to make the 90s carts pretty convincing.

But there was a very obvious hole.

The early 2000s.
I had almost nothing.

So I found another collection called `Commercials (Clean)`.
It contains almost two thousand commercials covering roughly 2000 through 2015.
The uploader had already trimmed and cleaned a lot of them, which is exactly what I needed.

I scanned that too.

There were hundreds of commercials just from 2000 through 2002.
I absolutely did not want to review another two thousand videos.
So I made a second version of the review page that only showed early-2000s individual-commercial-length clips.

It also had its own browser storage so I could not accidentally destroy the first finished review.

That would have been a very funny way to lose several hours of my life.

The new collection looked terrible at first.

After 76 clips I had only found 9 kid commercials.
I thought I had downloaded 20GB of insurance companies and SUVs.
Then it suddenly got good.

I stopped at 193 reviewed clips with:

```text
89 KIDS_00S
95 GENERAL_00S
2  PSA
7  SKIP
```

There are still 449 clips sitting there unreviewed.

I am leaving them that way on purpose.

I do not want to reuse commercials on the finished cartridges if I can avoid it, so that unreviewed pile is now reserve ammunition.

If I start running out later, I already know where to go.

At this point the commercial problem is basically solved.

Which meant it was time to move on to the thing that will probably make the fake channels feel even more convincing than the commercials.

Bumpers.
Idents.
"We'll be right back."
"Now back to the show."
"Up next."
Station IDs.

Little five-second pieces of television that nobody really thinks about until they disappear.

I was originally planning to hunt those down one network at a time.
Then I found a giant community collection of already-clipped bumpers.

And another archive.
And another.
I decided to keep this phase USA-only for now.

There are some fantastic Canadian YTV and Teletoon archives out there, but if I keep expanding the scope every time I find something cool, this television is eventually going to contain the entire history of broadcasting.

So I downloaded the American stuff.
Then I ran an inventory.

The folder is now about:

```text
131 GB
14,414 files
```

I have accidentally built a television archive.

The really useful branding collections alone contain more than five thousand files.

One of them has folders with roughly:

```text
1,700+ Cartoon Network bumpers
800+  Adult Swim clips
700+  Toonami clips
300+  Nickelodeon bumpers
200+  Fox Kids clips
100+  MTV bumpers
60+   Nick GAS clips
```

There is also SNICK material.

Kids' WB.
Cartoon Cartoon Fridays.
Cartoon Network Groovies.
Show-specific bumpers.
"Now back" bumpers.
"Up next" clips.
Promos.
Station IDs.

All kinds of weird little pieces of dead television infrastructure.

This is fantastic.

It also means I do not need to manually cut all of this stuff out of three-hour VHS recordings.
A lot of it is already separated and named.

Eventually I will make another review tool for this pile too.

The filenames are structured well enough that I should be able to auto-classify a lot of it into things like:

```text
NETWORK

NICK
SNICK
CN
KWB
FOX
TOONAMI
ADULT_SWIM
MTV
NICK_GAS
```

and:

```text
TYPE

IDENT
BUMPER_IN
BUMPER_OUT
NEXT
PROMO
INTRO
SIGN_OFF
OTHER
```

Then I can just preview the good candidates and keep the ones that fit the stations I am building.

The ridiculous part is that downloading all of this also created several future stations almost by accident.

I now have enough Toonami material that a Toonami cartridge seems inevitable.

Same with Adult Swim.

MTV is suddenly possible.

Nick GAS is sitting there asking questions.

I am trying very hard not to order more flash drives.

For now.

The project is in a really good place to stop for a few days.

The hardware works.
USB cartridges work.
I know the encoding format the television likes.
The twenty-cart lineup exists.
The episode manifests exist.
The 90s commercial library is curated.
The early-2000s commercial library is curated enough to use.
There is a giant reserve pool if I ever need more commercials.
And I now have more station branding than I know what to do with.

The next major step will be turning all of these ingredients into an actual automated cart builder.

Something that can take:

```text
station
episode list
commercial pool
bumpers
idents
```

and spit out a numbered, correctly encoded fake television broadcast that fits on one of the 4GB drives.

Then I can finally stop building infrastructure for fake television and actually watch fake television.

Probably.

I started this project because I wanted to put different videos on a novelty TV.

I now have 131GB of archival television infrastructure.

This seems proportionate.
