I Started An Interdimensional Record Label

Well, sort of.

This started because I have a pile of old music.

Not just finished songs. Half-finished songs. Demos. Ideas. Things I recorded twenty years ago and never really did anything with. Songs where I still remember what I *wanted* them to become even if I never actually got them there.

Then AI music tools got good enough that I could start feeding some of that stuff in and hearing completely different realizations of it.

That was weirdly emotional.

Not because I thought the AI had somehow resurrected my old band or uncovered the One True Version of a song. It was more like hearing a possibility I never got to hear the first time around.

What if I had arranged it differently?

What if somebody else had written that part?

What if I had actually finished the damn thing?

And then I had the thought that turned this from "Shawn makes AI covers of his old songs" into something much stupider and much more interesting.

What if the different versions were the point?

So I made a fictional record label.

Cosmic Raven Records.

The basic premise is that I accidentally built a weird homemade receiver. At first I just call it a radio because I have no better word for it.

Except it does not seem to tune into stations.

It tunes into possibilities.

At least that is what fictional me thinks is happening.

I can feed it a song I know, turn the dial, and somewhere in the static there is another version of it. Sometimes it is dramatically different. Sometimes it is almost the same except for a melody, a guitar sound, a vocal inflection, or one little decision that somehow changes the whole thing.

The important part is that I do **not** want the story to establish a giant hard-science cosmology where I explain exactly how alternate realities work.

The narrator is guessing.

Maybe it is alternate realities.
Maybe it is timelines.
Maybe it is possibilities.

Maybe the machine is doing something completely different and this idiot with a soldering iron has absolutely no idea.

That last one feels pretty believable.

I have been calling the whole thing a **found-media music fiction project**.

A slightly fancier version would be:
> A transmedia fiction project built around music recovered from alternate realities.

It has some ARG DNA, but it is not really an ARG. Nobody has to solve a cipher to hear the next song. I am not trying to convince anyone that I genuinely have an interdimensional radio in my basement.

The fiction is the presentation.

Every song gets archived as a transmission.

```text

TRANSMISSION 001A

TRANSMISSION 002A

TRANSMISSION 002B

TRANSMISSION 003A

...

```

The number is the underlying song, signal, or experiment.

The letter is another stable version I recovered.

That became useful almost immediately because I kept ending up with multiple versions of the same songs that I liked for completely different reasons.

Instead of choosing one and throwing the others away, now that indecision is canon.

Hell yes.

The first few transmissions are mostly fictional me having an existential crisis about the machine.

That part came pretty naturally because there is a real question underneath the fiction that I find interesting.

If I hear a musical idea generated from something I wrote, and then I take that idea back into my own music, whose idea is it now?

Is that inspiration?

Is it cheating?

If the fiction says another version of me wrote it, am I stealing from myself?

There is no big sermon or answer hidden in the project. I just think the question is interesting, so the narrator gets to wrestle with it too.

Then I started expanding the machine.

The original setup was just the radio and an input signal. I started calling the amount of original source being fed into it the **Anchor**. More source keeps the recovered version closer to what I know. Lower the Anchor and things start to drift.

Then fictional me decided hearing the signal was not enough and connected an old CRT television to the mess.

This was supposed to make the interference easier to see.

Naturally it made everything worse.

Controls on the television that should have absolutely nothing to do with the music started changing the music.

That gave me a few more homemade terms:

```text

Anchor

Drift

Depth

Stability

```

They are not supposed to sound like official science terms. They are literally just words some guy writes in his notes because he needs names for the weird behaviors he keeps seeing.

That distinction matters to me.

I want the machine to grow along with the story instead of retroactively revealing that I secretly built the fucking TARDIS in Transmission 001.

It was a radio.

Then it was a radio with a TV connected to it.

Now he mostly calls the whole mess the receiver.

Eventually I want using it to become almost mundane.

That is actually one of my favorite parts of the idea.

Early on, every signal is impossible and terrifying.

Later I want fictional me saying shit like:

> Stability was garbage so I backed the depth off and found it again twenty minutes later.

Like this is just what he does on a Tuesday now.

That also solves another problem I had.

A lot of the music I have made with AI is serious.

Some of it is absolutely not.

I have stupid songs. Comedy songs. Things that should not exist. Things that probably qualify as crimes against genre conventions.

At first I was worried that putting those next to the serious transmissions would wreck the tone.

Then I realized that is backwards.

If the receiver can find music from effectively anywhere, why would every reality have impeccable taste and a grim cinematic aesthetic?

Somewhere out there, somebody made the stupid song.

We found it.
We release what we find.
That is way more fun.

The newest part of the story is fictional me finally giving in and deciding to actually study the receiver instead of constantly whining that he needs to stop using it.

Transmission 005B is deliberately 005**B**.
There is no public 005A.
I am not explaining that yet.
Maybe I never will.

I like that the archive can have holes in it.

Transmission 006 starts getting more procedural. I have three versions of the same song, so the story is moving into a controlled repeatability test: same source, same settings, same process, and somehow the receiver still does not quite return to the same place.

That gives me 006A, 006B, and 006C without having to pretend each version is a completely separate event.

The YouTube presentation has become its own little project too.

I wrote a Windows BAT file around FFmpeg that turns a WAV into the full transmission video and thumbnail.

The command is basically:

```bat

make_transmission.v4.bat "Song.wav" "006A" "Artist Name" "Track Name"

```

It builds a five-second acquisition intro, stitches the intro audio and song together, draws the Cosmic Raven HUD, generates an audio spectrum, adds transmission metadata, renders the MP4, then creates a YouTube thumbnail.

The current videos are intentionally a little sterile and broadcast-y. Static background, HUD, signal status, visualizer, archive metadata.

I already want to redesign it.

I also absolutely do not want to redesign it right now.

Those are both true.

There were, naturally, a bunch of dumb little Windows and FFmpeg problems along the way.

Parentheses in filenames taught me exciting new things about CMD parsing.

FFmpeg taught me that colons are apparently dangerous.

A receiver timer taught me that sometimes the right feature is the one you delete.

And native FFmpeg progress output taught me that I would rather parse its machine-readable progress with PowerShell than watch Windows Terminal fill itself with twenty-seven wrapped copies of the same status line.

Eventually the generator reached the magical software-development milestone known as:

> Okay, quit fucking touching it for a minute. It works.

So naturally I have now put the code under Git and written a full doomsday rebuild guide.

The rebuild notes cover the folder structure, FFmpeg installation, exact asset names, the audio-stitching pipeline, the thumbnail generator, the visualizer settings, the Windows BAT landmines, transmission numbering, and the narrative rules I am likely to forget.

Because I know myself.

Five years from now I will absolutely open this folder and say:

> What the fuck is `.crr_combined_audio.wav`?

Now Future Me has documentation.

You are welcome, asshole.

The actual story text lives in a Google Doc called `Cosmic Raven Records - Transmission Script`.

That has turned out to be really useful because I can treat it as the single source of truth for descriptions and drafts instead of having slightly different copies scattered through chats, text files, and YouTube.

Pinned comments have also become part of the format.

I originally thought of them as teasers for the next transmission, but I like them much better as **later annotations**.

The description is what fictional me believed when he archived the signal.

A pinned comment added later can say, basically:

> I went back through my notes. I was wrong about this.

That means the old posts can continue participating in the story without rewriting history every time I come up with a better explanation.

I am being very explicit outside the fiction that this uses AI.

Every transmission includes this:

> About Cosmic Raven Records:  

> This is a fictional music and worldbuilding project using a mix of human-created and AI-generated music, visuals, writing, direction, editing, and curation. The transmissions, artists, histories, and alternate realities are part of the fiction.

> This is all an experiment.

I do not think hiding the tools would make the project more interesting.

The fun part is what I am doing *with* them.

So that is Cosmic Raven Records so far.

A fake interdimensional record label.
A music archive.
A story told through YouTube descriptions and comments.
A pile of old songs getting strange second lives.
A growing collection of fictional artists and realities I have not even gotten to yet.

And one increasingly comfortable idiot sitting in front of a radio and an old television, turning the dial to see what else is out there.

There are a bunch of obvious next steps.

The video presentation could use more motion and better backgrounds. I want genre metadata. I want a better archive system as the catalogue grows. Eventually I want to figure out Shorts that feel like actual recovered signal fragments instead of advertisements for the full videos.

But I am deliberately not disappearing into infrastructure right now.

The machine works.

I have songs to release.

So I am going to keep turning the dial.
