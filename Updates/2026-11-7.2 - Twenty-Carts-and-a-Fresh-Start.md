The twentieth cart is built. Version one of Retro TV Cart Builder has done its job.

I now have the first collection of twenty carts finished. What started as a way to assemble television for a little retro TV became a fairly involved operation: shows, commercials, bumpers, promos, cropping, audio repair, reviews, and finally getting the whole thing onto a flash drive. Somewhere in there I also started watching the builds through Jellyfin, because apparently a tiny television needed a distribution department.

## The final four

The last stretch settled into two Sci-Fi carts and two HBO carts. Sci-Fi paired original-series Star Trek with The Twilight Zone. HBO paired Tales from the Crypt with Spawn, giving those two shows more room across multiple episodes instead of trying to squeeze in a little of everything.

Finding the station branding was part of what made these work. The Sci-Fi idents and promos brought that strange late-night channel atmosphere, and the HBO openings and show promos tied the final pair together. I also got posters made for all four to use in Jellyfin.

The HBO commercials took another lap. I had a compilation of old adult hotline ads that needed splitting into three individual clips, then another eight separate downloads to give the second cart more choices. I thought I was running out of commercials again. Some were genuinely already used, but others were sitting in the library and simply weren't appearing as replacements.

That turned out to be a filtering problem. The cart configuration allowed several commercial pools, but Studio still restricted replacements to the current slot's pool. A general commercial slot couldn't see the new late-night ads. Fixing that for HBO2 got the choices back where I could use them.

There was also a small mess involving nested branding approval data, and another involving adding a closing clip without adding the draft entry Studio expected. Both got sorted. The final carts have their own short custom test-pattern endings. They aren't historical HBO sign-offs; they're endings for my carts.

HBO2 is now built. That's twenty.

## V1 earned its retirement

The program works, but using it for twenty carts gave me plenty of time to find the awkward parts. Too many commands. Too many little setup scripts. Rules that existed in configuration but didn't behave consistently in the interface. Too much waiting around for a render when I already knew I needed to change a crop.

I don't regret getting the collection finished with it. I have the carts, and I have a much better understanding of the program I actually want.

I moved the old files into a `legacy-v1` folder in the existing repository and started v2 separately. I'm building this incrementally with ChatGPT, reviewing each piece before adding the next. No enormous overhaul disappearing over the horizon and returning with an application I don't recognize.

## A project should own its own mess

The first v2 milestone is a desktop window with native folder pickers. I can create a project, add source library folders, save its settings, and open it again. I tried the initial build and have no complaints so far.

The storage model is the important part. Each project owns its data, artwork, composites, previews, builds, and exports. My media libraries remain source folders. The program references them instead of scattering its own files beside the episodes and commercials.

Media usage will also belong to the project by default. Using an episode or commercial in one collection won't automatically lock it out of another collection. Cross-project locking will be an explicit option when that part is implemented.

Next is networks and shows: names, artwork, and assigning a show to one or more stations. Carts, color-coded templates, library organization, generation, and Studio follow after that. Those are the plan, not features I'm pretending are already finished.

Twenty carts done, a working beginning for v2, and a sensible place to stop for a minute. It's been a long day, but a productive one.
