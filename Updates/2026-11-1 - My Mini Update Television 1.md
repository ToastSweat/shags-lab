# Fake Television, Real Commercial Breaks

I have spent a slightly unreasonable amount of time making television worse on purpose.

I have the episodes. I can pick something and watch it whenever I want. Apparently that isn't enough, because what I actually want is a few hours of cartoons interrupted by old commercials, network bumpers, and someone telling me the show will be right back.

The USB TV carts are getting there. Nickelodeon, SNICK, and Cartoon Network have been joined by two Kids' WB carts, FOX1 has gone into its build, and DISNEY1 is now assembled and open in Studio for review.

## The bits between the shows

The Kids' WB carts ended up being particularly satisfying. I split them into earlier and later flavors, with Animaniacs and Pinky and the Brain connecting the two. Freakazoid and Earthworm Jim fit the earlier cart; Tiny Toons and Batman give the later one a different personality.

Finding the branding made a bigger difference than finding another episode. An opening, a ridiculous little anvil bumper, and the right promo can make a playlist feel like an actual programming block.

FOX1 moves into The Tick, Batman, X-Men, and Spider-Man. There is some overlap between the networks, but different episodes and branding help each cart keep its own identity.

I'm also trying very hard not to repeat commercials, episodes, or branding across carts. That means checking what is already reserved or used, rather than trusting that a big folder must still have something available. A hundred clips stop looking like a lot after filtering out repeats and the stuff I don't actually want to watch.

## A Disney Afternoon after dark

DISNEY1 is DuckTales, Rescue Rangers, TaleSpin, and Darkwing Duck, with three episodes of each. I picked standalone stories so I don't spend the end of the cart wondering where the other half of an adventure went.

The missing piece was Disney Afternoon branding. I eventually downloaded a bumper compilation and had it split into 65 individual clips. The cuts used black transitions and audio silence, with a visual check to catch an internal black flash that would have split one animation in half.

I reviewed the suggested eleven. They all say the Disney Afternoon will be right back, so they belong **before** the commercials. A small distinction, but putting that announcement after the ad would be a very convincing recreation of me operating a television station while tired.

A recreated opening and a full closing sequence finish the package. The closing even has Mickey rolling up the backdrop. That is considerably more graceful than how most of my projects end for the night.

## The carts escaped into Jellyfin

I also set up Jellyfin, which gave the finished cart files another job. I can keep the USB versions and copy the finished clips into named folders for watching around the apartment, including on my phone.

Naturally, this immediately required a set of matching retro posters. Large cart numbers, loud colors, worn VHS-cover textures. The collection is starting to look like a shelf instead of a directory full of files.

The current builder still has some awkward edges. I'm finishing these carts with the working system, then I want to rebuild it in a fresh repository with a proper Library Manager, Cart Builder, and TV Studio. Most of the useful machinery already exists. Making it easier to use is the next project, not a reason to interrupt this one again.

For tonight, Disney is through setup and into review. Final build, USB export, and its Jellyfin copy are the remaining steps. Then I can finally watch some television I have painstakingly made less convenient.
