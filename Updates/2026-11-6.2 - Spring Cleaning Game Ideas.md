I spent tonight going through old game ideas. Mental and digital spring cleaning. Some were barely more than a title, some have art, and a few got far enough that there might still be a game hiding in the project files.

Apparently my younger self thought every project needed multiplayer, an enormous map, or an MMO attached to it. Sometimes all three. I have since discovered the appeal of finishing things.

I don't feel bad about putting these away. I wanted to see which ones still sparked something when I opened the box.

## The old boxes

There was **Butt Bricks**, originally Armstops: a Minecraft-like world made of LEGO-style pieces, with real part IDs and official colors. An endless bucket of bricks to build with friends. Lovely idea. Absolutely enormous amount of work. That one can rest.

There was pinball, because I wanted to make pinball. A medieval SimTower. Raft with PvP and MMO ambitions, followed by Raft in space. A VR lightsaber game that would teach the techniques. A fighting game where people could draw their own characters and make Dean Venture fight Daria, or whoever else they felt like drawing.

**18-Wheeler Santa Slayer** was Twisted Metal with semi trucks and crazy holiday characters. I have art for that one. **Space Penguins from Space: Traveling to the Future to Alter the Past, Somehow** was a third-person command-post shooter in the spirit of classic Battlefront II, with penguins and polar bears. The title was doing considerable work.

Then there was a five-by-five-mile island with a grand Slenderman mystery, car chases, and a scope that was completely out of hand. I started making heightmaps, and I still love the idea of making that island. Maybe there's a cryptid game in its future. I would enjoy building the place itself.

**Tit-tris**, where clearing lines removed a lady's clothes, is going in the scrap. I still want to make lewd Steam games someday. I can come up with something better than that.

## The ones that still have a pulse

The space trucker keeps calling me back. The Long Drive, but with a big-ass spaceship that looks like a semi. Repair it, maintain it, stop for fuel and supplies, find parts, and pick up more trailers. That and my ToeJam & Earl-inspired idea are practically screaming for my **Cosmic Raven** characters and assets.

**Wizzodango** is another old concept I still like: being a dog, peeing, pooping, farting, fighting, and generally doing dog things. I've developed that idea a little already. It belongs in the keep pile.

**Leap Year** is the definite one. Hand-drawn 2.5D with Garth, my farting arcade robot from Cosmic Raven. Some Cuphead inspiration, art closer to Don't Starve Together, and monochrome artwork so the palette and music could change during levels. I must finish that game. It's on my bucket list.

I also came back around to generating a game from a song. Two directions caught my interest: a physics-based stunt course and a destruction run. Feed it a track and see what happens to the terrain, obstacles, or city. Those can sit for a while, but I want to keep both possibilities.

## Some of this already existed

I got surprisingly far with a pedometer app for walking fantasy maps. Walk to Mordor, that sort of thing. The medals weren't the attraction for me; I liked the journey. Getting it working properly on phones was difficult, and Unity was probably the wrong tool for that particular job. I don't walk much anymore, but the concept still appeals to me.

I'd also love to find the right theme for a location-based game using WRLD. Pokémon Go, Wizards Unite, Ingress, that general kind of exploration. I haven't found the theme yet.

**Roadtrip Simulator** might be worth recovering. I got pretty far, but I wasn't good enough at coding at the time. It had some Desert Bus inspiration, except it was arcade driving with obstacle stages that gradually got harder, leading into an endless sort of mode. One day I want to give Hermes the existing project, see what actually works, and find out whether it could become something polished. I haven't done that inspection yet.

The other memory that stuck was a minigame I made in Multi Theft Auto. You drove a bulldozer, pushed rocks into a crusher, and got materials: copper, silver, gold, all the way up to opal. Opal had an absurd one-in-a-billion chance. The payouts weren't huge, but people loved grinding it.

I'd enjoy making something like that again, probably in GTA V. By the end of the conversation I was starting to think a GTA V server might be a good home for that kind of thing.

Nothing got built tonight. I haven't selected the game I'm going to spend the next ten years making, either. I have a clearer idea of what I can archive, what I might rescue, and what I still need to finish.

Spring cleaning complete. Garth remains my problem.
