# The Sith Order: Actually Beginning

Apparently the next project in the lab is a religion.

I've been fascinated by the Sith for years, especially Naga Sadow. Somewhere along the way, that fascination became a question I kept coming back to: what would it mean to take the passion, drive, and pursuit of mastery seriously in my own life?

On November 2, I decided to start finding out. Today I am a Sith. What that means in practice is something I'm going to discover.

And yes, I know how crazy this sounds. Bear with me, there is nothing but good intentions here. No Compounds, no special punch or Kool-Aid.

## Getting ahead of myself

I started by working through my beliefs and an interpretation of the Sith Code. Passion gives life meaning. Strength is resolve and dedication. Power is ability: knowledge and skill developed through effort. Victory can be finishing something difficult, overcoming an obstacle, or reaching a goal I chose for myself.

Cruelty and exploitation have no place in what I'm building. Rest matters. Failure happens. Asking for help takes responsibility. There is room to understand the Force metaphorically or spiritually, and room to admit I don't know.

That produced a founding statement and commentary on the Code that I was happy with. Then the discussion drifted into membership, ranks, leadership, succession, and procedures.

Eventually I had to stop and ask who the hell these rules were for.

It's literally just me.

I still like the idea of a community called The Sith Order. People sharing ideas, supporting each other, exploring spirituality, and occasionally being weirdos in robes with lightsabers sounds pretty good. But I was getting ahead of the part where I actually live this myself.

I archived the drafts and started a founder's journal. The ideas are there to revisit. For now, I'm beginning with firsthand experience.

## Something I already recognize

One of my first reflections was about making things.

I once spent a year making a thirty-minute animation by myself. I wanted to finish it, prove I could do it, and give people something they might enjoy. Publishing my first game on Steam and getting my current job brought similar feelings: all that effort had taken me somewhere I once wasn't sure I could reach.

The animation also taught me that achievement and recognition don't necessarily arrive together. Its release initially fell flat. Getting it noticed took more work. Then an online magazine in Brazil picked it up, the YouTube views exploded, and I felt incredible.

I was already proud of finishing it. Having people actually see it made that achievement feel validated.

I've wanted that response again plenty of times since. But I still make what interests me. People are welcome to enjoy it, and when they do, I appreciate it a lot.

That gave me something real to reflect on: how passion, persistence, pride, and recognition work in my own life.

## Consulting a holocron

Then I tried something that felt particularly appropriate: an AI roleplay of a Naga Sadow holocron.

I can't visit an ancient Sith teacher, but I can use a fictional conversation to examine my own thinking. I wanted him honest and challenging, while receptive enough to have a conversation with a guy from a universe where Star Wars is fiction.

It worked well for me.

The conversation asked what I expected to gain from being Sith if the Force didn't exist in my universe. My answer was the drive, passion, and ability I admired. That brought the discussion around to what I was actually going to do with those convictions.

Leading by example starts with myself.

Those are commitments I've made. Following through is the next part.

## Where this goes

I'm interested in meditation, symbolism, robes, candles, and exploring a lightsaber as a ritual tool. Those things may find a place as I learn what feels meaningful.

I also want to give the path time to develop. I don't need to invent a pile of ceremonies and responsibilities just to make the beginning look more complete.

For now, I'm keeping a journal, examining my choices, and finding out whether these beliefs help me act on things that matter to me. I'll talk about it with the right people and see where those conversations lead.

I have plenty of unanswered questions. After years of thinking about this, I'm finally giving myself some experience to work with.
