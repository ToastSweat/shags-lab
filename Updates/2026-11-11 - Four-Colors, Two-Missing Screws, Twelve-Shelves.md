I finally installed the ELEGOO CANVAS upgrade on my Centauri Carbon. It took all night and carried on into the next day, with plenty of trips between the printer, the manual, and installation videos.

Some steps were not entirely clear, and I ended up redoing parts of the installation after figuring out what the instructions actually meant. There were moments where buying a printer that already did multicolor sounded pretty damn appealing. On the other hand, upgrading what I already owned saved me roughly $400–$500. That is how I am choosing to look at it.

I only lost two screws in the bottom plate. Considering how long I spent taking things apart and putting them back together, I am counting that as restraint.

## Getting it talking

One pleasant surprise was finding the CANVAS data port already on the back of my printer. I preordered this machine, so I had expected that part of the upgrade to involve more work. Apparently it had been waiting for this longer than I had.

Once everything was installed and the firmware updated, loading filament became the next obstacle. My purple ELEGOO PLA would go a few centimeters into the inlet and stop. The feeder would not grab it, the Load button stayed gray, and all four inlet lights flashed red together. Trying a second slot produced the same result.

I eventually got past that stage and reached the first test print. Then the printhead started catching on the lever that opens the new waste chute.

## The chute was getting blamed

The replacement chute came as a complete assembly. Its angled gray lever appeared to be in the right position, and comparing it with the original did not reveal an obvious difference. Even pushing the lever back did not provide enough clearance for the printhead housing.

I spent a while trying to understand how these parts could possibly occupy the same space.

The breakthrough was finding a screw that prevented the printhead from traveling all the way forward. It was stopping partway through the homing movement. That explained the apparent position mismatch when it subsequently traveled back toward the chute: the printer was working from a bad reference.

Once that obstruction was dealt with, things started making a lot more sense.

## The other nozzle setting

After getting it printing, I briefly thought something else was wrong.

I was using a 0.4 mm nozzle setting with a 0.2 mm nozzle installed.

That one was mine.

After correcting the setting, the prints started coming out immaculate. There is a considerable difference between troubleshooting a machine and asking it to print with the nozzle you actually put in it.

## My first multicolor masterpiece

With CANVAS working, I now have to decide what I actually want to print in multiple colors.

Naturally, I have started printing twelve shelves. In one color. At about seventeen hours each.

That is roughly 204 hours of shelf printing assigned to my newly upgraded multicolor printer. I have plenty of more interesting projects in mind, but there is also a lot of existing work to finish, and the shelves are useful.

I also asked ELEGOO whether I could buy just the two cover pieces from the newer CANVAS kit and use them on the original Centauri Carbon. Support said they would not fit. I am not entirely convinced, but that question can wait. Compatibility is still unverified, and I am not buying another complete CANVAS kit just to investigate two pieces of cover.

For now, the printer works, the prints look excellent, and I can stop dismantling it long enough to make something.

Even if that something is twelve shelves.
