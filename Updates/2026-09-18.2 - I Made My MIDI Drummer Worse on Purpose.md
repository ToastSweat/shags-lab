I write a lot of my drum parts in Guitar Pro, I'm not saying it's the best, but when you've used something for 25+ years and it just works it's kind of hard to switch.

It is less great when I export the MIDI, drop it into REAPER, and discover that my drummer has apparently been replaced by a machine with atomic-clock timing and perfect control over every muscle in its body. I know metal drummers are precise but not everyone is Gene Hoglan.

Every hit is exactly where I told it to be, every note is perfectly obedient.

It sounds fake AF.

Obviously there are already ways to "humanize" MIDI. Move notes a little bit. Randomize the velocity. Make everything slightly wrong.

But that started bothering me too, because humans aren't just random.

A drummer doesn't have four identical sticks floating around the kit. They have two hands, two feet, a dominant side, a weaker side, and a limited number of limbs that can only be in so many places at once.

So I made a REAPER script that tries to make my fake drummer worse at drumming in a more realistic way.

The goal was pretty simple.

I wanted to export a finished drum part from Guitar Pro, bring it into REAPER, select the MIDI item, run one script, and have it come out sounding less like Guitar Pro.

Not random. Human.

The first part was figuring out what every MIDI note actually represented.

So I made a drum map:

```lua
[35] = "Kick Drum",
[38] = "Snare Drum",
[42] = "Closed Hi-Hat",
[49] = "Crash Cymbal",
[51] = "Ride Cymbal",
```

And so on.

Then I gave every drum piece a default limb.

```lua
[36] = "DF",  -- Dominant Foot
[38] = "DH",  -- Dominant Hand
[42] = "NDH", -- Non-Dominant Hand
[44] = "NDF", -- Non-Dominant Foot
```

That was the important part.

Instead of looking at a MIDI note and saying: "Move this somewhere around here."

The script can look at it and say: 
"This is a snare hit. That is probably the dominant hand."
"This is a hi-hat. That is probably the other hand."
"This is a kick. Foot."

Now we're cooking.

Once every note has a limb, the four limbs can have their own personalities.

In `Configuration.lua` I gave them separate strength values:

```lua
dominateHandStrength = 99
nondominateHandStrength = 91
dominateFootStrength = 93
nondominateFootStrength = 87
```

So the dominant hand is naturally stronger than the non-dominant hand.

The feet aren't identical either.

Then they each get their own timing bias, plus a little random variation.

```lua
velocityRandomRange = 10
timingRandomRange = 2
```

That means the "humanization" isn't just one random number being thrown at every note.

A snare hit and a hi-hat hit can drift differently because they came from different simulated limbs.

The same goes for velocity.

Which is a very complicated way of making a computer slightly worse.

Perfect.

There was another problem though.

What happens when the MIDI asks one hand to hit two things at the same time? Or three?

The script groups notes that occur together and tries to resolve limb conflicts.

If two notes both want the dominant hand, one of them can be reassigned to the non-dominant hand.

The same basic idea applies to the feet.

There is also logic for alternating limbs through sequences instead of having one imaginary arm perform an entire drum roll like a woodpecker.

That part made the whole thing feel much more interesting to me.

I wasn't just adding noise anymore. I was starting to model a person.

A very simple person. With four limbs. Who lives inside REAPER.

The script also has a list of MIDI notes that I don't want in a standard drum-kit part.

Things like bongos, agogos, claves, vibraslap, cowbells, metronome hits, and a bunch of other percussion are marked separately from the kit pieces I actually expect.

So while analyzing the MIDI it can flag things that don't belong in the kind of drum track I'm trying to process.

The actual program ended up split into a few modules:

```text
Main.lua
Init.lua
Mapping.lua
AnalyzeMidi.lua
ProcessMidi.lua
```

`Main.lua` is basically the conductor.

It initializes the script, loads the mappings, analyzes the MIDI, and then processes it.

`AnalyzeMidi.lua` is where the interesting drummer-brain stuff happens.

It reads the MIDI notes, assigns limbs, groups simultaneous hits, alternates limbs where appropriate, checks for conflicts, and builds an internal representation of the performance.

Then `ProcessMidi.lua` takes that information and actually changes the MIDI.

Velocity gets adjusted around the configured strength for the limb.
Timing gets shifted according to that limb's bias plus some randomness.
Then the original MIDI notes are replaced with the processed ones.

And REAPER says:

```text
Humanization Complete!
```

I also apparently started making a GUI for this.

But that's a project for another day. Maybe.

The current version in the repository is `0.9`, which feels appropriate because there are definitely still rough edges.

There are checks I would tighten up.
The configuration could be much friendlier.
The GUI obviously went wherever unfinished GUIs go to die.

And there is a lot more that could theoretically be modeled if I ever wanted to get ridiculous with it.

Tempo.
Fatigue.
Accents.
Ghost notes.
How far a hand has to move across the kit.
Different playing styles.
Different actual drummers.

That last one is probably the most interesting possibility.

The configuration is already structured around the idea of an emulated player's timing and strength.

So instead of having one generic "humanize" button, there is at least the beginning of a system where different configurations could represent different feels.

Not random imperfection.
Specific imperfection.

Which I think is the thing I like most about this project.

Computers are extremely good at doing exactly what you tell them.

Music often sounds better when they don't.
