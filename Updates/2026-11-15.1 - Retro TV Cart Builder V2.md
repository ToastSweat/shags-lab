I can finally call the second version of Retro TV Cart Builder a working program. I finished the build and USB-copy workflow, tested it on my setup, and committed and pushed the code. The software for deliberately putting commercial breaks back into my life has reached another milestone.

The first version already made the original twenty carts. It worked, but getting there involved a lot of scripts, handpicked files and knowledge that mostly lived in the conversation. I wanted a desktop application where the project itself held the organization and I could do the ordinary work without asking which PowerShell command came next.

## A project instead of a pile of instructions

V2 starts with creating or opening a project. Networks, shows, artwork, carts, templates, classifications and review progress belong to that project. My media libraries stay where they are, and the app leaves the originals alone.

I can organize shows and commercials in bulk, browse the actual folders, and mark material classified so it drops out of the remaining work. With more than seventeen thousand indexed files in this project, that last part matters. One clip at a time is a fine way to spend the rest of my natural life sorting cereal commercials.

Cart templates also support preferred choices and alternatives. A slot can try one kind of bumper first and fall back to another, and those choices carry through to the replacement pool during review. Saved lineups reserve their media within the project, so I can keep track of what is already being used.

## Keeping the part that already worked

The Studio took an unnecessary detour through a redesign. It looked good, but familiar controls and review indicators had gone missing or moved. I had already spent plenty of time tuning that workflow. A prettier screen was not a good trade for having to relearn where everything lived.

I went back to the original Studio files as the reference. The timeline has its review states and colors again, the controls are back in their familiar places, and the original crop, trim and audio-repair behavior is retained. I can also change a crop and restart processing while a preview is still rendering.

Good + next means I have approved the clip. It does not mean I now need to watch the whole thing again in a different window and create another revision to prove that I meant it.

## Back onto the cartridge

The final piece was connecting local builds and USB copying. Approved previews get reused, the finished clips get clean numbered filenames, and copying verifies the files before reporting completion. Size fitting is an explicit choice rather than a surprise reduction in quality.

The automated suite finished at 76 passing checks, including real FFmpeg processing and simulated copy failures and rollback. More usefully for me, the actual workflow is working on my machine.

I left Jellyfin out of this application. That was a useful side project, but it did not need to become another requirement standing between me and a finished cart builder. Composite creation and deeper duplicate detection can wait too.

For now, I have a working checkpoint saved in Git, documentation to go with it, and considerably fewer commands to remember. I am accepting the win before this turns into another entire television network.
