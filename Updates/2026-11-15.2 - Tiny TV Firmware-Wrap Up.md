I had to go to sleep so I broke this into two parts.

One last update before I put the screwdriver down.

I opened the woodgrain mini TV to investigate changing its built-in USB screen, charging animation and other interface graphics. Replacing the ordinary videos is easy. Getting control of the little operating system underneath them is another matter.

The board confirmed an **Actions V100** processor and Micron NAND storage with an 8 GB nominal capacity. I also recorded the board and LCD ribbon markings and made an image of the entire USB-exposed disk. That preserves the accessible storage, but it is **not a verified firmware backup**. The image analysis did not identify the built-in screen graphics.

Attempts to find a USB service mode produced a brief file-list screen and, with a different button held, a repeating battery-screen/black-screen cycle with clicks from the speaker. Turning it off and unplugging it restored normal operation. That was enough button roulette.

Research turned up related V100 investigations and a supplier advertising customization tools, but no verified firmware readout method for this TV. I found the AliExpress seller for my other three units and sent a request for the factory software, original firmware and instructions for changing the built-in graphics. That request has been forwarded to the seller; no technical answer yet.

I'm parking the investigation here with the woodgrain working normally, its exposed storage backed up, and its hardware identified. I haven't unlocked the firmware or changed those screens.

If I return to the hardware side, I may build my own larger-screen TV instead. There are also VHS-shaped USB cartridge enclosures to design in the coming weeks. Apparently my response to a stubborn tiny television is to consider becoming a television manufacturer.
