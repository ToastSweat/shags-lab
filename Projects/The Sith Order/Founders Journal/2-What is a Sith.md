What is a Sith? What should it mean? What could it mean?

I feel closest to being a Sith when my passion and drive take me somewhere I never thought I could reach. Making something I’m proud of gives me a powerful feeling: I did this. What else could I do? That feels so powerful.

I spent a year making a thirty-minute animation by myself. I kept going because I wanted to see it finished, prove to myself that I could do it, and give the world something people might enjoy. Publishing my first game on Steam and earning my current job gave me similar feelings. The hard work, late nights, and frustration had led somewhere.

But finishing the animation didn’t feel the way I had imagined. When I released it, the response fell flat. Getting it noticed became another task. I was proud of what I had accomplished and disappointed that it wasn’t reaching people.

Then an online magazine in Brazil picked it up, and the YouTube views exploded. That was when I felt incredible. The achievement was already mine, but now it felt validated. People were seeing something I had worked so hard to create.

I’ve wanted to experience that level of interest again for years. It has only happened a few times. Appreciation matters to me, especially when I’ve made something I want to share.

Still, I follow my own interests when deciding what to create. I make what I like, and people are welcome to enjoy it. When they do, I appreciate it a great deal.

For now, this is something I recognize in myself: passion gives me a reason to begin, dedication helps me finish, and sharing the result gives others a chance to connect with it. I can be proud of the work and still wish it had reached more people. Both feelings are part of my experience.

I felt like sharing that, I feel like it shows the passion of a Sith
