## The continuous core

The strongest full-archive continuity belongs to handles that appear near the start and remain visible close to the end.

**Shagwrath** has the largest surviving footprint and is present across all eleven calendar years represented by the archive.

**Steven** and **Juby** appear on the first day and remain present into 2026.

That does not mean those three people were always equally active or that nobody else was equally important socially. It means the surviving text gives them unusually continuous coverage.

## The early Discord cast

The first years heavily feature **Headygr, PcBro Stig, Choco, Amir077, SlayerZim, Tempa, Juby, Steven**, and one now-anonymous **Deleted User** whose 8,000+ messages make the loss of identity historically significant.

## The bridge era

**Soul** becomes a major textual presence beginning in 2018.

**Tilong** appears in 2020 and becomes increasingly intertwined with servers and shared games.

**Yuu** is technically an early-era name but becomes particularly visible again in the Grounded/Valheim/modern-game periods.

**Byars** is also an old Discord-era name whose later activity connects the early and modern communities.

## The later cast

**fart in a jar moosey** first appears in the surviving record in 2022 but becomes one of the dominant voices of 2025–2026.

**L i n k a n ツ** arrives in the modern era and quickly becomes part of the contemporary cast.

This is typical of PGN: newer members enter a community that still contains enough old names and old references to absorb them into existing lore.

## The deleted-user problem

One deleted identity produces more than eight thousand surviving messages and is especially prominent in 2016–2018.

If old screenshots or personal memory can identify that account, it would materially improve the early history.

## Better than "old guard vs new guard"

PGN does not show one clean generational replacement.

It shows overlapping cohorts: early Discord regulars, people who fade and return, later central members, game-specific recruits, project collaborators, server regulars, and people who may barely type but remain socially present.

That messy overlap is probably more important to durability than a stable formal roster would have been.
