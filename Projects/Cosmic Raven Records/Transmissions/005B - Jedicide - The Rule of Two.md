# TRANSMISSION 005B [Jedicide, The Rule of Two]

[Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dUxBkdX4Ae0)

I’ve been thinking about this all wrong.

For weeks I’ve treated the receiver like something I needed to resist. Something I should only touch for a few minutes before putting it away again.

That clearly isn’t working.

So I’m done pretending I don’t want to know what this thing can do.

I still don’t know if any of this is right. I’ve just stopped pretending that uncertainty is going to stop me.

I built an impossible machine.

Maybe it’s time I actually used it.

The radio and television are more or less permanently connected now. The workbench is a disaster. Wires everywhere. Notes taped to everything. Marks around the dials. Pages of settings that probably only make sense to me.

I’ve started giving the behaviors names.

Anchor.
Drift.
Depth.
Stability.

They aren’t technical terms. I don’t know enough to have technical terms. They’re just the words I keep writing in my notes because I needed some way to describe what I was seeing.

Anchor is the easiest.

It’s basically how much of the source I’m feeding into the receiver.

Drift is what I call the movement away from it.

Depth is harder.

The television gave me controls I didn’t have before, and tiny adjustments can push a signal somewhere completely different.

Not just a different performance.
Sometimes a different genre.
Different instruments.
Different lyrics.
Different everything.

Stability is the least mysterious. It’s just my shorthand for how well a signal holds together once I find it. How much snow is left on the screen. How much static is in the audio. Whether I can take my hands off the controls without losing it.

I’ve found things over the last few days that I don’t have words for yet.

Some of them are incredible.
Some of them are completely ridiculous.

And I’ve realized something.

I don’t need every signal to make sense.
I don’t need to decide which version is best.
I don’t even need to know what I’m looking for.

I just want to know what’s out there.

I can sit there for hours now, barely touching the controls, waiting for the static to change.

You start hearing patterns in it.

A rhythm buried underneath the noise.
A note that shouldn’t be there.
A voice for half a second.

Then you move the dial a fraction and suddenly there’s a whole song on the other side.

That still gets me.

Every single time.

There is so much out there.

More than I could ever listen to.
More than I could ever understand.

And for the first time since I built this thing, that thought doesn’t bother me.

It makes me want to keep turning the dial.
