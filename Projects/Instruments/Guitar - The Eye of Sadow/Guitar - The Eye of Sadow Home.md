## Marketing
Sitting at the top of Cort's G Series when it debuted in 2001, the G290 was built as a premium modern take on the classic double-cutaway electric guitar. Rather than simply dressing up an entry-level instrument, Cort equipped the G290 with the kind of components normally associated with considerably more expensive guitars: genuine Seymour Duncan pickups, Sperzel locking tuners, Wilkinson hardware, a carved figured-maple top, and a birdseye maple neck.

This 2001 example is finished in striking Cherry Red Sunburst, transitioning from a vivid red outer edge through orange and into a bright amber center. The transparent finish highlights the deeply figured quilted maple top, while a matching burst headstock carries the same dramatic appearance through the entire instrument.

Underneath the carved quilted maple top is a lightweight swamp ash body, combining the resonance and clarity of ash with the visual depth of highly figured maple. Its sculpted double-cutaway shape gives the G290 the familiar comfort of a Strat-style guitar while sharper horns and a more modern silhouette give it an identity of its own.

The bolt-on birdseye maple neck is paired with a rosewood fingerboard and 22 frets on a traditional 25.5" scale. At the other end of the neck, a Wilkinson roller nut works together with Sperzel Trim-Lok locking tuners and a Wilkinson VS50 II tremolo to provide a remarkably sophisticated tuning system for an early-2000s production guitar.

A genuine Seymour Duncan HSS pickup set gives the G290 a broad tonal range. Two Vintage Staggered-style single-coils provide the clear, open tones expected from a traditional Strat configuration, while a hot Seymour Duncan TB-4/JB-family bridge humbucker adds the power and output needed for hard rock, metal, and high-gain lead playing.

With a five-way selector and coil-tap functionality, the G290 can move from bright single-coil cleans and glassy combination tones to a full bridge humbucker without sacrificing the simplicity of its controls.

The result is a surprisingly high-end instrument from a period when Cort was building some exceptionally well-equipped Korean guitars. With its premium aftermarket-grade hardware, genuine Seymour Duncan electronics, figured woods, and bold finish, the first-generation G290 represents an unusually ambitious entry in Cort's early-2000s lineup.
### Carved Quilted Maple Top
The visual centerpiece of the G290 is its carved quilted maple top. Rather than using the flatter, more traditional construction found on many Strat-style guitars, Cort sculpted the top and allowed the figuring of the maple to become a major part of the instrument's design.

The Cherry Red Sunburst finish emphasizes that figuring with a vivid red perimeter that fades through orange into amber and yellow toward the center.

Beneath the maple is a light swamp ash body, giving the instrument a resonant and relatively lightweight foundation.
### Birdseye Maple Neck
The G290 uses a bolt-on birdseye maple neck, a visually distinctive upgrade over the more conventional maple necks found on many guitars in its price range.

A rosewood fingerboard provides a smooth playing surface with 22 frets and a full 25.5" scale length, maintaining familiar Strat-style string tension and response.
### Seymour Duncan HSS Pickup Set
Unlike many similarly priced guitars of the period that used licensed or house-brand pickups, the first-generation G290 was equipped with genuine Seymour Duncan electronics.

Two Vintage Staggered-style single-coils occupy the neck and middle positions, providing traditional clear and articulate single-coil tones.

At the bridge is a Seymour Duncan TB-4, the trembucker-spaced version of the legendary JB. Its higher output gives the G290 a significantly more aggressive voice when needed, making the guitar equally comfortable moving from clean and bluesy sounds into hard rock and metal.

### Versatile Electronics
A five-way pickup selector provides access to the G290's HSS pickup combinations, while an additional coil-tap function expands the available sounds from the bridge humbucker.

The simple one-volume, one-tone layout keeps the controls uncluttered while still giving the guitar considerably more tonal range than a basic HSS configuration.

### Wilkinson Roller Nut and Tremolo
One of the most unusual features of the early G290 is its Wilkinson roller nut. The strings ride across individual rollers rather than dragging across conventional nut slots, reducing friction when the tremolo is used.

The nut is paired with a Wilkinson VS50 II two-point tremolo, providing smooth pitch control without the complexity of a double-locking Floyd Rose system.

This hardware combination was replaced on later versions of the G290, making the roller-nut first-generation instruments particularly distinctive.

### Sperzel Trim-Lok Tuners
At the headstock, Sperzel Trim-Lok locking tuners provide fast string changes and additional tuning stability.

Combined with the Wilkinson roller nut and tremolo, the locking tuners create a high-quality tremolo system that remains simple to restring and adjust.

### First-Generation G290
The G290 was introduced in 2001 as the flagship of Cort's G Series, and this guitar dates to February of that first production year.

Later G290s underwent substantial changes. Around 2005, Cort moved to an EMG HSS pickup configuration, replaced the Wilkinson tremolo with its CFA-III system, and replaced the Wilkinson roller nut with a Graph Tech nut.

That makes this 2001 instrument part of the original Seymour Duncan/Wilkinson specification that defined the first-generation G290.
