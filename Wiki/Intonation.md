# Intonation

Intonation is how well an instrument stays in tune as you move up the neck. An open string can match the tuner perfectly while notes farther along the fingerboard sound sharp or flat. Ordinary [[Wiki/Tuning|Tuning]] sets the open string; intonation adjustment fine-tunes its working length, usually by moving a saddle on the [[Wiki/Bridge|Bridge]].

A useful check compares the fretted note at the twelfth fret with its octave reference. Pressing too hard can pull the note sharp, so the test needs a normal playing touch, not a death grip.

During [[Bass VI - Penny - First Time Setup|Penny's first setup]], Shag explicitly left intonation alone because the bass was playing well. The notes do not claim a full adjustment was performed. New strings can justify checking it, especially after a big change in string type or thickness, but checking and changing are different things. Good [[Wiki/Action|Action]] and a sensible neck setup help too.
